# Asimov v1 assurance, explained

[Asimov v1](https://github.com/asimovinc/asimov-v1) is an open-source bipedal humanoid by Asimov Inc.: a 1.2 m,
35 kg robot with 25 actuated joints, a tele-operation stack on a Raspberry Pi 5 in the head, and a motion control
board on a Radxa CM5 that is the last computer before the actuators, published as mechanical CAD, electrical CAD and
a simulation model. This project forks it and adds what a machine needs before it may work near people and what
upstream, by its own account, does not have: a risk assessment, safety functions with a required Performance Level,
a deterministic safety layer, a hardwired stop chain, an electrical installation that can be switched off, a position
on the learned locomotion policy, a cybersecurity case, and a safety case that publishes its own counter-evidence. The
standards are the machinery set with the robot standards: ISO 12100, ISO 10218-1/-2 with ISO/TS 15066, ISO 13482,
ISO 13849-1, IEC 60204-1, IEC 62133-2, IEC 62443, ISO/IEC TR 5469 and the Machinery Regulation.

This wiki is the orientation layer. It explains how the project is organised, how the work flows through its
phases and gates, which standard shapes which part of the record, and where to look. It never states a safety
claim: those live in the [assurance record](../assurance/README.md), where they are reviewed.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Asimov v1 is research hardware and this record is a draft prepared for engineering review.** Nothing has
> passed verification review, confirmation review or a functional safety assessment. The robot is not CE-marked,
> no safety function reaches any Performance Level today, and no configuration is approved for operation around
> people. A biped adds one thing a wheeled machine never asks: removing power drops the robot, so even the safe
> state is still a decision for the lead.

## The safety argument in one picture

![Platform architecture in three layers: the inherited tele-operation stack and the learned locomotion policy, hardened as defence in depth; the deterministic safety layer on the motion control board as the last software before the motors; and a hardwired stop chain as the checker of last resort. The safety layer and the stop chain are the only layers with a Performance Level target. A cross-cutting assurance column lists the record that argues it.](../docs/assets/asimov/architecture.svg)

*The tele-operation stack and the policy propose, the safety layer on the motion control board checks, and a
hardwired stop chain that nothing in software can override is the checker of last resort. Only the safety layer and
the chain carry a PL target; the tether and gantry of the laboratory carry the fall while no fall-arrest function
exists. The argument is
[WP-M-01 §6](../assurance/01-management/WP-M-01-assurance-strategy.md#6-safety-architecture-argument-the-central-strategy);
[Reading the safety case](Reading-the-safety-case.md) explains how the claims, evidence and defeaters are recorded.*

## What this wiki is not

- **Not a work product.** Nothing here carries a document-control header or counts as evidence.
- **Not the place for hazard, requirement or safety-function text.** Each page links to the record that owns it.
- **Not illustrated by hand.** Every image is a figure generated from the repository's records, or rendered from
  them at sync time.
- **Not edited in place.** Pages are kept in the repository under [`wiki/`](../wiki/README.md), reviewed by pull
  request, and mirrored here on merge. An edit made on the wiki itself is overwritten by the next sync.
